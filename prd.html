Product Requirements Document

ARISE ICT HUB

Candidate Registration & Certificate Verification Portal

A public web portal for ARISE ICT HUB, Uyo. Prospective students enrol online and receive an auto-generated registration number; graduates verify their record and download an official PDF certificate using two factors. An allowlisted admin console manages candidates and issues certificates — with no privileged key ever reaching the browser.

1.Overview

1.1 Problem

ARISE ICT HUB enrols students onto ICT programmes and awards a certificate on completion. Both processes have been manual: registration on paper, and certificates handed out physically with no way for a student — or a prospective employer — to confirm afterwards that a record is genuine. Paper records are slow to search, easy to lose, and impossible to verify remotely.

1.2 Solution

A single responsive web portal that does four things:

  • markets the hub and its programmes (landing and about pages);
  • takes student registrations online and issues a registration number automatically;
  • lets a student verify their record and download their certificate using their registration number plus the phone number they registered with;
  • gives staff an admin console to review candidates, add historic (paper-registered) students, and issue or revoke certificates.

1.3 Goals

  • Digitise enrolment — replace the paper form with a validated online one.
  • Make certificates verifiable — any holder can prove their record from any device.
  • Protect candidate data — personal records must never be publicly enumerable.
  • Keep admin work small — one upload per student issues a certificate.
  • Preserve history — previously registered students can be brought into the system.

1.4 Non-goals (this release)

  • Payments, invoicing, or fee collection.
  • Course delivery, lessons, assignments, or grading (no LMS).
  • Student self-service login and accounts — verification is credential-based, not session-based.
  • Automated certificate generation or templating — the PDF is produced outside the system and uploaded.
  • Bulk/CSV import of historic students (added one at a time via the admin form).
  • Email or SMS notifications.

1.5 Users

UserNeeds from the product
Prospective student Understand the programmes, then register from a phone in a few minutes and receive a registration number to keep.
Enrolled student / graduate Check whether their certificate has been issued and download the official PDF without creating an account.
Administrator (allowlisted staff) See who has registered, add students who enrolled on paper, upload each certificate, and revoke one issued in error.
Third party (employer, institution) Confirm a certificate is genuine — possible only with the holder's own two credentials, by design.

2.Scope & Site Map

The product is a single-page React application with five routes. Public pages share a marketing header and footer; the admin console renders outside that chrome with its own shell.

RouteAccessPurpose
/Public Landing page — hero, statistics, programmes, why-choose-us, how it works, testimonials, sponsors.
/aboutPublic Mission, story, core values, offerings, strategic goals, call to action.
/registerPublic New student registration form with passport photo upload.
/verifyPublic Certificate inquiry — two-factor lookup, student profile, certificate download.
/adminAdmin only Console: dashboard, students, certificates, settings.
Routing note

Unknown paths fall back to the landing page. The host is configured to rewrite all paths to index.html so deep links such as /verify resolve on a hard refresh.

2.1 In scope

  • Public marketing pages with the hub's real programme and sponsor content.
  • Online registration with server-side validation and server-assigned registration numbers.
  • Passport photo upload (JPEG/PNG, maximum 5 MB).
  • Two-factor verification: registration number + registered phone number.
  • Certificate download via a short-lived signed URL (five minutes).
  • Admin console with dashboard statistics, searchable/filterable student list, profile view, certificate issue and revoke, and manual student creation.
  • Full mobile responsiveness including a bottom tab bar and bottom-sheet modals on the console.

2.2 Out of scope

  • Everything listed under non-goals (§1.4).
  • Multi-role admin permissions — every allowlisted admin has identical rights.
  • Audit logging of admin actions beyond the derived activity feed on the dashboard.
  • Editing a candidate's details after creation.

4.Key User Flows

4.1 Register as a new student

  1. The applicant opens /register from the site header or a call to action.
  2. They complete the form and attach a passport photo, seeing a preview before submitting.
  3. The photo uploads to public photo storage under a random file name.
  4. The client calls the registration procedure, which re-validates every field, rejects a duplicate email, derives the current year, and assigns the next number in sequence.
  5. The applicant sees a confirmation screen with their registration number and is told to keep it with the phone number they used.

4.2 Verify a record and download a certificate

  1. The student opens /verify and enters their registration number and phone number.
  2. The verification procedure matches both factors, comparing the phone on digits only, and returns a narrow profile — or nothing.
  3. The profile renders with the photo, personal details and status. If no certificate has been issued, a pending message shows instead of a download button.
  4. If issued, the student presses download. The signing service re-checks both credentials, confirms the certificate exists, and mints a five-minute signed URL.
  5. The browser saves the PDF as an attachment without leaving the page.

4.3 Issue a certificate

  1. The admin signs in at /admin; the database confirms allowlist membership.
  2. They filter the students list to Awaiting and open a candidate.
  3. They attach the candidate's PDF (≤ 5 MB) and confirm the upload.
  4. The file is written to private storage as {candidate_id}.pdf.
  5. The issue procedure verifies the file is present, then sets the certificate path, verified flag and issue date. If the file is missing the call fails and the status is unchanged.
  6. The student's download unlocks immediately on the verify page.

4.4 Add a paper-registered student

  1. The admin presses the floating + button on the students tab.
  2. They complete the add-student sheet, including the original registration year, phone number and passport photo.
  3. The photo uploads, then the admin-only creation procedure re-checks the allowlist, validates every field, and assigns a fresh number for that year.
  4. The list refreshes, the new profile opens, and the certificate can be uploaded straight away.

5.2 Supporting records

  • Registration counters — one row per year holding the last sequence issued, keeping numbers dense and collision-free.
  • Admin allowlist — the set of email addresses permitted to administer the portal. Unreadable from the browser; an admin is added by inserting their address after their sign-in account exists.

5.3 Storage

StoreVisibilityContents and rules
PassportsPublic Passport photos under random file names. Publicly readable so profiles render; uploads accepted during registration. JPEG or PNG, ≤ 5 MB.
CertificatesPrivate One PDF per student named {candidate_id}.pdf. No public access of any kind. Read and written only by allowlisted admins or the signing service; students receive five-minute signed URLs. PDF only, ≤ 5 MB.

6.Security Requirements

Governing principle

Never trust the client. The browser holds only a public key and, for staff, a signed-in session. Every rule that matters — who may read, who may write, what a valid record looks like, and what a registration number may be — is enforced by the server.

IDRequirement
SC-1There is no broad public read of the candidate table. Row-level security is on and denies by default; the public reaches data only through two vetted procedures.
SC-2Public reads require both the registration number and the phone number. The database cannot be enumerated with a registration number alone.
SC-3The public profile returns a deliberately narrow field set. Internal id, certificate path, email, address and date of birth are excluded.
SC-4Writes are only possible through vetted procedures. No table-level insert or update permission is granted to anonymous or signed-in users.
SC-5The certificate store is private with no anonymous access policy at all.
SC-6Certificate links expire after five minutes and are minted per request.
SC-7The privileged service key exists only as a server-side secret in the signing service. It is never bundled, exposed, or sent to a browser under any circumstance.
SC-8The signing service independently re-verifies both credentials before releasing a link — it does not trust that the caller already passed verification.
SC-9The signing service refuses to sign when a certificate has not been issued.
SC-10Admin authority comes from a database allowlist evaluated on the server for every privileged call. Modified client code cannot grant it.
SC-11The allowlist is unreadable from the browser.
SC-12Admin read access to candidates is scoped to allowlisted admins; anonymous visitors are never granted it.
SC-13Registration numbers are generated exclusively server-side by an internal routine the public cannot call directly.
SC-14Registration years are derived from the server clock for public registration; an admin-supplied year is range-checked.
SC-15File type and size limits are enforced for both uploads: images (JPEG/PNG) and certificates (PDF), each ≤ 5 MB.
SC-16Certificate issue is transactionally safe: the status cannot be set unless the file is confirmed present.
SC-17Error messages are generic. Failed verification never discloses which credential was wrong, and internal errors are never surfaced verbatim.
Accepted trade-offs

Passport photos are publicly readable. They sit under unguessable random file names, but anyone holding the exact URL can view the image. This is accepted so profiles render without a signing round-trip; the URL is only disclosed after a successful two-factor lookup.

Verification is credential-based, not rate-limited in the application. Guessing requires a valid registration number and its matching phone number together. Adding request throttling at the edge is a recommended follow-up.

Every admin is equal. There are no tiers; anyone on the allowlist can add students and issue or revoke certificates.

7.Technical Design

7.1 Stack

LayerChoice
ApplicationReact 18 with TypeScript, bundled by Vite; a single-page app with client-side routing.
StylingOne hand-authored stylesheet using design tokens; no CSS framework.
IconsA single icon library used consistently across the site and console.
BackendManaged Postgres with row-level security, authentication, object storage and edge functions.
Certificate signingA dedicated server-side function — the only holder of the privileged key.
BuildType-check then bundle. The build fails on any type error or unused symbol.
HostingStatic hosting with a single-page-app rewrite rule.

7.2 Server-side entry points

Entry pointCallerResponsibility
Register candidatePublic Validate, reject duplicate email, derive the year, assign a number, insert the record, return the number.
Verify candidatePublic Match registration number and phone (digits only); return the narrow profile with a computed age, or nothing.
Certificate signingPublic Re-verify both credentials, confirm the certificate is issued, return a five-minute signed download link.
Issue certificateAdmin Confirm the allowlist, confirm the file exists, then set path, verified flag and issue date.
Revoke certificateAdmin Confirm the allowlist, then clear path, verified flag and issue date.
Add candidateAdmin Confirm the allowlist, validate as for public registration plus a year range check, assign a number, insert, return id and number.
Admin checkAdmin Report whether the signed-in address is on the allowlist.
Why procedures rather than direct table access

Row-level security denies everything by default and no write permission is granted to any browser-facing role. Each procedure runs with elevated rights but only performs its one vetted operation, after re-validating its inputs. This makes the set of possible writes finite and auditable: the browser cannot compose an arbitrary query, only invoke one of these.

7.3 Configuration

  • The client is configured with the backend URL and the public key only. Both are safe to ship.
  • The privileged service key is injected into the signing function by the platform and is never present in the frontend build.
  • Schema changes are applied by running the schema file, which is written to be safely re-runnable.

7.4 Deployment checklist

  1. Run the schema file against the project database.
  2. Add each administrator's email to the allowlist after creating their sign-in account.
  3. Confirm both storage buckets exist with the correct visibility — passports public, certificates private.
  4. Deploy the certificate signing function.
  5. Build and deploy the frontend with the public configuration values set.
  6. Smoke test: register a candidate, verify it, issue a certificate, download it, then revoke it.

9.4 Success measures

  • The share of new enrolments completed online rather than on paper.
  • Successful verifications as a proportion of attempts (a low rate suggests students are losing their credentials).
  • Median time from programme completion to certificate issued.
  • Certificate downloads per issued certificate.
  • Registration form completion rate — starts versus submissions.
